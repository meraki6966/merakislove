What should a Portland pet owner watch for?

Seven things we see every year in this city, with the months they show up. The bar beside each one runs January to December.

Raw salmon and trout

JFMAMJJASONDSeptember to December

What you see
Vomiting, fever and swollen glands within six days of eating raw fish from a river or a cooler.
What to do
Call us the day it happens. Treated early with an antibiotic and a wormer, most dogs improve within two days. Without treatment, nine in ten sick dogs die.

Foxtails and grass seeds

JFMAMJJASONDJune to September

What you see
Sudden head shaking, sneezing fits, or licking at one paw after a walk through dry grass.
What to do
Call the same day. The barbed seed only travels one way, and it has to be found and removed.

Wild mushrooms

JFMAMJJASONDOctober to December

What you see
Drooling, vomiting or wobbling after time in the yard or on a trail once the fall rains start.
What to do
Go to the emergency hospital and bring a photo of the mushroom. Some kinds that grow here damage the liver.

Lilies

JFMAMJJASONDMarch to May

What you see
A bouquet or a potted Easter lily in the house. Every part is toxic to cats, including the pollen and the vase water.
What to do
Go to the emergency hospital right away, even if your cat seems fine. Treatment in the first hours protects the kidneys.

River algae

JFMAMJJASONDJuly to September

What you see
Green scum or paint like streaks on slow water. The Willamette around Ross Island gets advisories in late summer.
What to do
Keep dogs out of the water during an advisory. If your dog swam or drank and seems weak or is vomiting, go to the emergency hospital.

Antifreeze

JFMAMJJASONDNovember to February

What you see
A sweet tasting puddle under a car in the driveway or garage during the cold months.
What to do
Go to the emergency hospital right away. A very small amount is dangerous to a cat or a dog.

Fleas

JFMAMJJASONDAll year

What you see
Scratching, scabs along the back, or black specks in the coat. Portland winters are mild enough for fleas to live all year.
What to do
Keep prevention going every month, including for indoor cats. Ask us which product fits your pet.

Which signs cannot wait?

If you see any of these, skip the appointment line and go straight to an emergency hospital. After hours, that is DoveLewis in Northwest Portland, open 24 hours every day.

This list is general guidance and cannot examine your pet. If you are unsure, call us during the day or DoveLewis at night.

  • Trouble breathing, or gums that look pale, white or blue
  • Collapse, or a seizure that lasts more than a few minutes
  • A swollen belly with retching and nothing coming up
  • Hit by a car, even if they seem fine
  • Ate rat bait, antifreeze, xylitol gum, grapes or a lot of chocolate
  • Bleeding that will not stop
  • Trouble breathing, or breathing with the mouth open
  • A male cat straining in the litter box with little or no urine
  • Chewed or licked any part of a lily, even the pollen
  • Collapse, or a seizure that lasts more than a few minutes
  • A fall from a window or balcony, even if they seem fine
  • Licked antifreeze or ate rat bait

Are you taking new patients?

Yes. We see dogs and cats, and most first visits are booked within the week. A puppy or kitten first visit is forty minutes, so there is time for every question.

What should I do if my pet has an emergency after hours?

Go to DoveLewis Veterinary Emergency and Specialty Hospital at 1945 NW Pettygrove Street in Northwest Portland. It is open 24 hours every day, and the number is (503) 228-7281. If you think your pet ate something toxic, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center answers at (888) 426-4435 day and night. A consultation fee may apply.

How much does an exam cost?

A wellness exam is $78 and a same day sick visit is $95. Anything beyond the exam gets a written estimate first, and you approve it before we start.

Does my dog need a rabies vaccine in Oregon?

Yes. Oregon requires dogs to be vaccinated against rabies by six months of age. Multnomah County also licenses dogs and cats, and asks for a current rabies certificate. We hand you the certificate at the visit.

What is salmon poisoning?

It is an infection dogs get from a parasite in raw salmon, trout and other fish that run upstream, and it is most common west of the Cascades. Signs show up within six days. It is treatable when caught early, so call us the day your dog eats raw fish.

Is the clinic calm for cats?

We try to make it so. Cats have their own waiting nook away from the dogs and a quiet exam room at the back, and we book cat visits at the slower times of day when we can.
