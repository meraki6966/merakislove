What is near the inn?

Four places guests ask about before anything else, and one street they find on their own.

  • The East End Historic District

    The inn sits inside it. The district covers about 150 acres and more than 550 buildings, and it has been a National Historic Landmark since 1976. Most of the houses are Victorian, with a few Greek Revival survivors.

  • The Strand

    The old commercial street by the harbor, about half a mile from the inn. Its Victorian buildings hold shops, galleries and restaurants now.

  • The Seawall

    Built from 1902 to 1904 after the 1900 Storm, and extended ever since. It now runs for more than 10 miles along the Gulf, and the beach is a short bike ride from our porch.

  • The Bolivar ferry

    It is free and it runs 24 hours a day. The crossing is 2.7 miles and takes about 18 minutes each way.

  • Dickens on The Strand

    December 4 to 6, 2026, the 53rd year of the festival. Rooms for that weekend are the first to go. See the weekend.

The name

Why is Galveston called the Oleander City?

The name has been in print since at least 1874. That January the Galveston Daily News suggested that Oleander City suited the town better than Island City.

The streets were lined with oleanders then, and many still are. Ours bloom pink along the front fence from spring into fall.

Getting around

How do guests get around?

Most guests park once and leave the car until they go home.

  • By bicycle

    Six cruisers with baskets and locks wait by the side gate. They are free for guests, first come.

  • On foot

    The East End is flat and shaded, and the porches are worth the walk on their own.

  • By car

    Leave it in the gated lot behind the house. You will want it for the west end of the island.

  • By ferry

    Walk on or drive on at the east end of the island. The crossing to the Bolivar Peninsula is 2.7 miles and takes about 18 minutes.

The island, answered

How far is the beach from the inn?

A short bike ride. The Seawall runs along the Gulf side of the island, south of the inn.

Are the bikes free?

Yes. Six cruisers with baskets and locks are free for guests, first come.

Do I need a car in Galveston?

Not for the East End, The Strand or the nearest beach. You will want one for the west end of the island.

Is the Bolivar ferry free?

Yes. It is free and it runs 24 hours a day. The crossing is 2.7 miles and takes about 18 minutes each way.
